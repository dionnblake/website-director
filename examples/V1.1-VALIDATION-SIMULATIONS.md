# V1.1 VALIDATION SIMULATIONS: THE DESIGN DIVERSITY TEST

> **Status:** Planning-Only Simulations — No websites were built for these three businesses.
> **Purpose:** Prove that Visual Research + Motion Direction produce materially different outcomes for materially different businesses, rather than converging on one fashionable "AI portfolio" look.
> **Rule Being Tested:** If all three converge toward cinematic hero + dark mode + giant typography + monospace labels + parallax + editorial minimalism, V1.1 has failed the diversity test.

These are reasoning simulations only: Discovery Input → Research Plan → Research Synthesis → Design-Direction Recommendation → Motion-Level Recommendation, run through the actual V1.1 protocols. None of these three businesses exist; no `projects/` folder was created for them, and nothing here touches `projects/alpha-starts-now/`.

---

## TEST A — Premium Dental Practice

### Discovery Input
Boutique cosmetic/restorative dental practice. Affluent, image-conscious adult patients who are anxious about dental visits and have been burned by impersonal "franchise dentistry." Primary conversion: book a consultation. Anti-brand: "Don't look like a cheap franchise dental chain. Don't look like a cold clinical hospital portal. Don't look like a generic healthcare SaaS product."

### Research Plan (`research-brief.md`)
- **Mode:** `full` — meaningful competitive set exists, worth the full pipeline.
- **Industry landscape (10–12 competitors):** Studied a mix of franchise dental chains and boutique cosmetic practices.
- **Landbook:** Searched hero, navigation, and imagery categories for "healthcare," "wellness," and "boutique service" patterns.
- **Cross-industry candidates:** Boutique hospitality/spa (calm, ritual, being cared for) and fine dining (warmth, unhurried attentiveness) — explicitly *not* law or architecture, which would read cold and institutional for a business whose core anxiety to overcome is fear of clinical coldness.
- **Deep-recon targets (2):** One boutique spa site (for its unhurried pacing and warm imagery direction) and one non-franchise dental practice that had visibly escaped the industry template.

### Research Synthesis (`research-synthesis.md`)
- **Industry Reality:** Patients arrive anxious; they scan for "does this feel safe and human" before anything else.
- **Industry Clichés:** Stock-photo close-ups of unnaturally white teeth; corporate blue-and-white color schemes; smiling-stranger stock photography; circular badge icons for every service ("Whitening," "Implants," "Braces") in a repeating 3-card grid.
- **Best-in-Class Lesson:** The one escaped-template dental site succeeded by using real, warm, natural-light photography of the actual space and staff instead of teeth close-ups — trust came from seeing real people and a real room, not from clinical iconography.
- **Cross-Industry Lesson (from spa/hospitality):** Unhurried pacing and generous whitespace communicate "you will not be rushed here," which is the single biggest anxiety the audience has.
- **Design Opportunity:** Nearly every competitor uses cold clinical blue or sterile white. A warm, daylight, human palette is unclaimed territory in this specific competitive set.
- **Principles Rejected:** The boutique spa reference's heavy use of ambient looping background video was rejected — a dental practice showing an empty, ambient room on loop reads as vacant rather than warm; real work only reads as care when a person is doing it.
- **Originality Check:** Recommendation draws pacing from hospitality and photographic honesty from the one strong dental outlier, but the resulting palette, typography, and section structure match neither source directly.

### Design-Direction Recommendation
- **Archetype Blend:** 60% Boutique/Artisanal + 30% Organic/Botanical + 10% Editorial.
- **Palette:** Warm neutrals — oatmeal, soft clay, warm ivory — explicitly **light-mode**, daylight-toned.
- **Typography:** Humanist serif display (warmth, not authority) paired with a clean grotesque body face.
- **Imagery:** Real natural-light photography of the practice and staff; zero stock teeth close-ups.
- **Explicit divergence from Alpha Starts Now:** ASN's validated direction is 60% Modernist + 30% Architectural + 10% Editorial, dark-mode-native (`#0C0E12` obsidian base), with a restrained monograph/heritage-journal posture built for a disciplined 35–50-year-old male audience. This recommendation shares only the 10% Editorial accent with ASN — the dominant Boutique/Organic blend, the light warm palette, and the humanist (not geometric-modernist) typography are deliberately different because the two audiences need opposite emotional postures: ASN needs authority and restraint; this practice needs warmth and safety. If this recommendation had drifted toward a dark modernist monograph look, that would indicate the archetype selection was reused from the last project rather than derived from this one's research — which is exactly what this protocol exists to prevent.

### Motion-Level Recommendation
- **Level:** `MOTION_LEVEL_1` (Subtle).
- **Rationale:** The audience's core need is to feel calm and unrushed, not impressed. Section reveals and gentle hover feedback support that; a cinematic hero or scroll-driven canvas sequence would read as the practice showing off production value instead of bedside manner — it would fail the Motion & Life QA dimension (`QA-RUBRIC.md` §5.2) by adding spectacle with no justification against any of the Six Motion Justifications.
- **Cinematic specialist:** Not engaged. `motion.cinematic_specialist_required: false`.

## TEST B — Architecture / Interior Design Studio

### Discovery Input
Boutique architecture and interior design studio, mid-size residential and hospitality projects, portfolio-driven. Primary conversion: inquiry for a consultation on a new project. The studio has extensive real photography and renders of completed work. Anti-brand: "Don't look like a generic WordPress portfolio theme. Don't look like a construction contractor site."

### Research Plan (`research-brief.md`)
- **Mode:** `full`.
- **Industry landscape (12 competitors):** A wide split between genuinely excellent studio sites (full-bleed photography, minimal chrome) and generic portfolio-theme sites (identical thumbnail grids, no narrative).
- **Landbook:** Searched hero, editorial-structure, and section-transition categories for architecture/interior portfolio patterns.
- **Cross-industry candidates:** Fine-art gallery sites and high-fashion editorial spreads, for full-bleed imagery discipline and restrained typographic hierarchy that doesn't compete with the photography.
- **Deep-recon targets (2):** One top-tier studio site (for its scroll-driven project narrative structure) and one fashion-editorial site (for its image-to-type ratio discipline).

### Research Synthesis (`research-synthesis.md`)
- **Industry Reality:** Visitors are evaluating taste and craft before they read a word of copy — the imagery has to do the persuading.
- **Industry Clichés:** Uniform card-grid portfolios with identical crop ratios; generic "Our Process" 4-step icon rows; stock architectural-blueprint backgrounds behind hero text.
- **Best-in-Class Lesson:** The top-tier studio site used a sticky-narrative structure — a pinned project image with copy scrolling past it — that let one photograph carry an entire section instead of being reduced to a thumbnail.
- **Cross-Industry Lesson (from fashion editorial):** Extreme restraint in type size and placement makes the imagery read as more expensive; when type competes with the photograph, both lose.
- **Design Opportunity:** Nearly every local competitor still uses generic card grids for project display, despite having strong photography to work with — the whitespace here is narrative sequencing, not more content.
- **Principles Rejected:** The fashion-editorial reference's aggressive full-viewport image cropping was rejected for project photography specifically, because architecture clients need to see complete spatial relationships (a cropped room reads as evasive, not artful).
- **Originality Check:** The sticky-narrative mechanic and editorial restraint are both principles, not compositions — this studio's actual projects, palette, and section count are unique to its portfolio.

### Design-Direction Recommendation
- **Archetype Blend:** 60% Architectural + 30% Editorial + 10% Modernist.
- **Palette:** Light limestone and concrete neutrals with a single muted terracotta accent — **light-mode**, daylight-toned (not the dark obsidian of a technical/SaaS Modernist treatment).
- **Typography:** Clean geometric sans display with a serif editorial accent for project narratives.
- **Imagery:** Full-bleed real project photography as the primary content, sticky-pinned during scroll for key projects.
- **Note on shared archetype terms with other projects:** This blend shares the "Architectural" and "Modernist" labels with Alpha Starts Now's blend, but at different weights, in light mode instead of dark, with a warm limestone/terracotta palette instead of ASN's obsidian/orange, and built around real project photography rather than editorial typographic composition — proving that reusing an archetype name from the catalog does not mean reproducing another project's direction.

### Motion-Level Recommendation
- **Level:** `MOTION_LEVEL_2` (Cinematic Enhancement).
- **Rationale:** This is the legitimate Level 2 case: real, high-quality photography exists; the sticky-narrative and zoom-parallax mechanics found in research directly serve Storytelling and Hierarchy (two of the Six Motion Justifications) because they let one strong image carry a section instead of shrinking it into a grid thumbnail. This is a deliberate art-direction choice earned by the content on hand, not a default.
- **Cinematic production:** Not required — the sticky-stack and zoom-parallax mechanics are achievable with standard GSAP ScrollTrigger work against real client photography; a separate cinematic generation adapter has nothing to add when real project photography already exists. `motion.cinematic_specialist_required: false`.

## TEST C — Local Plumbing / Home Service Business

### Discovery Input
Family-owned local plumbing company, single service area, primary conversion: phone call or same-day booking. Small budget, small existing web presence. Anti-brand: "Don't look like a scammy 1-800 franchise site. Don't look like we're trying too hard."

### Research Plan (`research-brief.md`)
- **Mode:** `bounded` — a real competitive set exists (other local trades), but the project's scope does not warrant the full 10–15-competitor / deep-recon pipeline. Recorded in `site-profile.json`: `research.mode: "bounded"`, with the reason a real competitive landscape exists but is small and low-differentiation, so 6–8 competitors were reviewed instead of 10–15, and no deep-recon targets were escalated. This is a scoped-down pass, not a skipped one — no `research.exception` was needed.
- **Industry landscape (7 competitors reviewed):** Franchise home-service sites and a handful of other local trades in the same metro area.
- **Landbook / cross-industry:** Not run at full depth given the bounded scope; a brief look at other trusted local-trade sites confirmed the same clichés rather than surfacing new categories worth deep study.

### Research Synthesis (`research-synthesis.md`)
- **Industry Reality:** A visitor who lands here is often mid-emergency — they want to know in under 5 seconds whether this company is legitimate and how fast they can get someone on the phone.
- **Industry Clichés:** Red-and-blue franchise color schemes, clipart pipe/wrench icons, stock-photo handshakes, urgent all-caps "CALL NOW 24/7" banners repeated three times per page.
- **Design Opportunity:** Competitors are either cheap-looking template sites or over-designed franchise sites that feel corporate and impersonal for a family business. The whitespace is "obviously legitimate and fast, without feeling like a call-center."
- **Principles Rejected:** A cross-industry look at boutique hospitality sites (considered briefly, given ASN and the architecture studio both drew value from adjacent-sector research) was explicitly rejected here — a plumber aspiring to a boutique-hotel visual register would be dishonest positioning and would actively undermine trust ("why does a plumber's site look like a spa?"). Cross-industry borrowing is only valid when it serves the client's actual positioning; here it does not.
- **Originality Check:** The recommendation is plainer than either Test A or Test B by design — differentiation here comes from clarity and speed, not visual ambition, and forcing a more elaborate direction onto this business would itself be a homogenization failure (importing another project's ambition rather than serving this one's).

### Design-Direction Recommendation
- **Archetype Blend:** 60% Premium Corporate/Institutional (restrained, not elaborate) + 30% Industrial (utilitarian honesty) + 10% Modernist (clean CTA hierarchy).
- **Palette:** Clean neutral base with one confident accent color for the call-to-action — deliberately unglamorous.
- **Typography:** A single clean grotesque sans, no display serif — legibility and speed over character.
- **Imagery:** Real photos of the actual truck, actual team, actual local jobs — not stock handshake photography, not artistic renders.
- **Layout:** Phone number and "Book Same-Day Service" CTA visually dominant above the fold on every viewport; service area and reviews immediately below.

### Motion-Level Recommendation
- **Level:** `MOTION_LEVEL_1` (Subtle) — leaning toward `MOTION_LEVEL_0` on the mobile experience specifically.
- **Rationale:** The audience is often on a phone mid-emergency on a slow connection. Speed and immediate clarity of the call CTA outrank any atmospheric motion; simple button feedback and a single fade-in on load are the ceiling here, not a floor to build up from. A scroll-driven cinematic hero would directly work against the conversion goal by delaying the CTA behind decorative content and adding mobile payload this audience cannot afford to download.
- **This is the explicit negative-control case:** the cinematic specialist and `MOTION_LEVEL_2`+ are available capabilities in the system, but availability is not justification. `motion.cinematic_specialist_required: false`, and this file exists in part to prove that Website Director did not reach for the cinematic toolkit simply because it exists.

## Diversity Verification

| Dimension | Dental | Architecture | Plumbing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Archetype Blend** | Boutique + Organic + Editorial | Architectural + Editorial + Modernist | Premium Corporate + Industrial + Modernist |
| **Theme** | Light, warm | Light, limestone/concrete | Light, neutral/utilitarian |
| **Typography** | Humanist serif + grotesque | Geometric sans + editorial serif | Single clean grotesque only |
| **Hero Type** | Static/photographic, human-centered | Sticky-pinned project photography | Static, CTA-dominant |
| **Motion Level** | 1 (Subtle) | 2 (Cinematic Enhancement) | 1, trending to 0 on mobile |
| **Cinematic Specialist** | Not engaged | Not required (real photography sufficient) | Not engaged |
| **Cross-Industry Source** | Hospitality / fine dining | Fine art / fashion editorial | None warranted — explicitly rejected |

None of the three converge on a dark theme, a scroll-driven cinematic hero, monospace labels, or heavy parallax — the failure pattern this test exists to catch. Each recommendation traces to a specific, stated reason grounded in that business's own audience and content, and each explicitly names what it rejected and why. The one deliberate overlap (Dental and the Architecture studio are both light-mode) is coincidental to their respective audiences' needs, not a sign of convergence — and both are explicitly light-mode as a point of contrast against Alpha Starts Now's dark-mode-native direction, proving V1.1 does not default toward ASN's validated look simply because it is the one available worked example in this repository.
